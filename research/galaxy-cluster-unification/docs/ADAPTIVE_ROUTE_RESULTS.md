# Adaptive baryonic gravity-route results

## Question

Can the apparent dark-matter map be treated as an arrival map for gravity
generated by baryons, with spacetime redirecting part of an otherwise scalar
excess field along curved routes?

The tested weak-field form is

\[
\rho_g(\mathbf y)=\rho_b(\mathbf y)+\int d^3x\,[A(\mathbf x)-1]\rho_b(\mathbf x)
\{[1-f(\mathbf x)]\delta(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)+f(\mathbf x)P(\mathbf y|\mathbf x)\}.
\]

`A-1` is the scalar excess supplied by the P0554 potential/path law. `f` is the
routed fraction, and `P` is a normalized center-return route kernel built from
observed cluster-member positions and F160W light weights. Only the excess is
routed. The baryonic Newtonian/GR base remains at the baryons.

The map-selected A0279 rule uses

\[
f=\sigma[\operatorname{logit}(0.45)+z_{\rm extent}],\qquad
L=250\,\mathrm{kpc}(R_{50}/150\,\mathrm{kpc})^{1/2},\qquad w=60\,\mathrm{kpc},
\]

where `z_extent` combines member-light extent and concentration. The raw
angular strength is suppressed by `s_theta=f^p`.

## What survived and what failed

| Stage | Frozen or selected rule | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Ten-cluster lens-map transfer | 362 laws, leave one cluster out | A0279 beat fixed C0351 in 9/10 clusters; median gain 11.60% | Spatial extent contains transferable map-shape information, but seven different candidates won the ten folds. |
| Raw RX J2129, unit angular strength | A0279, no amplitude fit | 12.452 arcsec vs 1.348 scalar | Catastrophic failure. A normalized arrival map does not determine a safe deflection amplitude. |
| RX J2129 amplitude bridge | `p` selected from 15 training images | selected `p=0.5`; held-out 2.739 arcsec vs 1.357 | Training selected the wrong angular strength. Post-hoc `p=2.5` happened to improve held-out RMS by 7.45% and was not promoted. |
| Four-cluster raw replay | post-hoc `p=2.5` locked before scores | 19.160 arcsec, all 11 held-out roots | Failed the 2-arcsec target and was 1.886 times the compact-halo validation RMS. |

The four-cluster scalar parent failed one held-out MACS1931 root, so its
finite-only RMS cannot be compared as a four-system average. On the three
systems where both scalar and routed models had complete roots, the routed
field improved equal-system RMS by only **0.276%**. In the historical validation
subset only MACS1115 was mutually complete, and the change was **0.0018%**.
The defensible positive result is narrower: routing changed the MACS1931 lens
topology enough to recover the missing held-out image root. It did not make the
already-solvable clusters appreciably more accurate.

## What the real cluster variation taught us

The same formula generated very different routed fractions from the member
layouts:

| Cluster | Members used | `f_route` | `f_route^2.5` | route correction RMS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MACS0329 | 80 | 0.650 | 0.3405 | 0.422 arcsec |
| MACS0429 | 52 | 0.011 | 0.000014 | 0.000008 arcsec |
| MACS1115 | 58 | 0.289 | 0.0449 | 0.082 arcsec |
| MACS1931 | 92 | 0.449 | 0.1351 | 0.552 arcsec |

MACS0429 is an especially useful null: its low member concentration drives the
route almost completely off, and every amplitude variant is indistinguishable
from the scalar parent. MACS1931 carries the topology result. MACS0329 and
MACS1115 remain inaccurate at roughly 23--25 arcseconds.

The one-at-a-time perturbations were scored at the primary geometry. Many small
changes caused a nonlinear image root to appear or disappear. Comparing only
systems with roots in both directions gives these positional-RMS spans:

| Parameter | Matched systems | RMS span |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base routed fraction | 2 | 0.590 arcsec |
| Extent slope | 3 | 0.214 arcsec |
| Route width | 2 | 0.039 arcsec |
| Source-weight power | 2 | 0.022 arcsec |
| Route length | 2 | 0.004 arcsec |

Thus the amount routed matters more than the exact arc length in the current
translation. The larger apparent spans obtained by dropping failed roots are
not valid parameter effects. Root topology is itself highly sensitive, which
is scientifically interesting but also makes ordinary RMS tuning unreliable.

All constructed fields retain a negligible numerical curl (about `3e-17`) and
their circular monopole is removed. The inherited P0554 galaxy score remains
12.592 km/s, its CLASH summary score remains 0.199 dex, and its Solar-System
proxies remain passed. These are preservation statements, not new successes of
the angular route.

## Universal constraints now supported by the data

1. Route only the excess above baryonic GR. Routing the full baryonic field is
   unnecessary and makes compact-system recovery harder.
2. A normalized arrival-map shape does not fix angular field strength. A
   separate constitutive relation is required.
3. Member extent can predict broad map morphology, but a map-similarity win is
   insufficient to predict raw multiple-image positions.
4. The present center-return route mostly changes critical-curve topology; it
   does not reduce positional residuals in clusters that already have roots.
5. The routed fraction is the most consequential continuous parameter tested.
   Exact length, width, and source-light exponent are secondary at matched
   roots.
6. The current source map is incomplete: it contains cataloged galaxies but no
   accepted hot-gas surface-density map or a separately measured diffuse ICL
   component. That omission is a testable limitation, not an excuse to retune.

## What the completed backtracking says

The inverse launch-to-arrival calculation has already been run on ten RELICS
clusters with 832 strict photometric member galaxies. Balanced optimal
transport connected member-light launch points to the positive lensing-map
residual after subtracting a fitted local-light shape. Lenstool and GLAFIC path
maps agree well (median Pearson correlation 0.969 and Jensen--Shannon distance
0.0173). Both imply a typical minimum projected route near 90 kpc and a
cluster-median 90th percentile near 154--159 kpc.

That agreement shows the numerical backtracking is reproducible across the two
lens reconstructions. It does not show that the inferred lines are physical:
the real angular member layout beats radius-preserving shuffles in only 6/10
Lenstool clusters and 5/10 GLAFIC clusters. Much of the apparent route is
therefore explained by radial geometry. The strongest repeatable clue is that
extended member distributions have a larger inward-return share. That clue
generated the extent gate used by A0279, whose raw forward failure is reported
above. See [`GRAVITY_FLOW_INVERSE_RESULTS.md`](GRAVITY_FLOW_INVERSE_RESULTS.md)
for the origin tables, equations, path maps, and controls.

## Next decision gate: repeat the inversion with complete baryons

Repeat the transport reconstruction with baryonic stars, BCG/ICL, and X-ray/SZ
gas as separate launch distributions and raw-observable lens posteriors as the
arrival constraint. The existing galaxy-only inverse used standard-GR
convergence products and cannot distinguish missing gas from a nonlocal field.
Compress the new routes using only baryonic potential depth, tidal tensor,
component identity, source separation, and environmental boundary conditions.
Then freeze the resulting law and require:

- complete roots and less than 2-arcsecond raw strong-lensing RMS;
- held-out weak shear or magnification prediction from the same potential;
- transfer to untouched clusters without object-specific gravity parameters;
- unchanged galaxy and Solar-System controls; and
- a materially closer result than the 9.989-arcsecond limited compact-halo
  comparator on the current historical validation pair.

Until that gate is passed, the supported observation is: **baryon-derived
routing can alter cluster image topology, but this center-return law does not
yet explain cluster lensing accuracy.**
