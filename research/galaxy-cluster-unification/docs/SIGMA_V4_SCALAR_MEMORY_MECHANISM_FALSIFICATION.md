# Sigma v4 scalar-memory mechanism falsification

## Scope of the stop decision

Three materially distinct projected formulations have now failed the same
spent-map accuracy and cross-system transfer gates. The project therefore
stops modifying the **single-scale isotropic scalar-memory mechanism**. This
is a mechanism-level falsification, not a claim that every baryon-only theory
or every nonlocal theory is false.

No untouched observation was opened in these screens. The separate count of
action-derived raw topology failures remains two: Sigma v1 and Sigma v2.

## Three distinct closures

| Closure | Physical ingredient | Joint NRMSE | Main failure |
|---|---|---:|---|
| v4A | varied local STF tide versus one tensor memory | 0.903971 | correct sign but edge-localized; only 0.794% residual power removed |
| v4B | varied quadratic gravitational-vector stress versus one tensor memory | 0.882874 | broad spokes but wrong phase; one-scale transfer fails |
| v4C | positive baryon-seeded directional-disorder trace with one scalar memory | 0.814737 | central trace works for PLCKG287 but erases AS295's distributed shear geometry |

All three use the complete total baryonic field, no object label, no
per-cluster force parameter, and one universal isotropic Helmholtz memory. They
fail for different immediate reasons but at the same decisive level: none
predicts convergence and both shear components across the other cluster with
one frozen response.

## What is falsified

The following strategy is retired:

> Compress the total baryonic field into one local scalar or STF statistic,
> compare or propagate it with one isotropic finite-range memory, and recover
> the missing cluster Hessian using one shared amplitude.

Adding an exponent, changing the bounded activation, separating a second
isotropic length, flipping a sign, or choosing a scale per cluster would not
address the information loss. The source either differentiates structure into
edges/spokes or smooths it into a central monopole. It does not simultaneously
retain distributed multipoles and create broad trace curvature.

## What survives

Four observations remain useful:

1. A positive baryon-seeded trace can remove `37.05%` of PLCKG287's residual
   map power with no object-specific gravity parameter.
2. AS295 requires substantially more displaced/oriented information than an
   isotropic central response retains.
3. The independently preferred propagation scales differ by a factor `3.68`
   (`133.10/36.12`), even while the same source definition is used.
4. Broad convergence and transferable shear are separate physical demands;
   success on a spherical/radial profile cannot establish the latter.

## Required change of mechanism

The next theory lane must change the propagation law, not merely the scalar
source. A defensible candidate must contain both:

- a baryon-unique sourced trace channel capable of nonzero broad response; and
- an orientation-carrying transport or constrained tensor state that preserves
  distributed multipoles instead of replacing them with one isotropic blob.

The most direct theoretical target is an anisotropic Green operator whose
principal directions are derived locally from the baryonic tidal/field-stress
tensor, or a degenerate trace-free field dynamically forced by that tensor.
It must still have no arbitrary homogeneous halo mode, use one physical metric,
retain `c_T=c`, and expose no untouched data until its action, constraint count,
and spent-map phase-transfer test pass.

Before implementing another map fit, the project must derive:

1. physical postulates for the trace and orientation carrier;
2. the covariant or rigorously causal effective action;
3. the unique baryon-forced boundary prescription;
4. the weak equations for both massive matter and photons; and
5. the positive-mode, luminal-tensor, and high-field screening conditions.

This is the required rethink under the goal's three-failure rule.
