# P0618: universal route phase

P0618 asks whether the cluster-dependent sign found in P0617 is caused by one
globally wrong angular convention. It freezes the self-coupled amplitude, the
P0617 mildly widened support, the return length, and the smooth contrast. The
complete positive route map is then rotated by one angle shared by every
cluster before its annular monopole is removed.

Nine universal phases from -90 through +90 degrees span radial, oblique, and
tangential-like routing. Exact roots are scored at the five frozen geometries.
Selecting a different phase for each system is recorded only to diagnose phase
dispersion; it is forbidden as a gravity model. The galaxy and Solar results
remain inherited nulls of the angular layer.

## Result

The safety-first shared phase is +90 degrees. It retained all 18 roots,
improved 3/5 systems, improved the mean per-system response by 1.685%, and
improved RXJ2129 by 8.241%. MACS0329 (+0.116%) and MACS1115 (+0.614%) also
improved. MACS0429 (-0.320%) and MACS1931 (-0.226%) resisted the shared rule,
so the cross-system gate fails. A +67.5-degree phase had the largest mean gain
(1.877%) but a worse minimum response (-0.422%), which is why the safety-first
ranking chose +90 degrees.

Negative phases improved three of the four MACS systems but lost one of seven
RXJ2129 roots. Positive phases from 0 through +90 degrees retained every root.
The individually preferred phases were +90, -90, -67.5, -90, and +67.5
degrees. Because phase is spin-2, +90 and -90 represent the same axis; their
spin-2 resultant is 0.883. This is evidence that a tangential-like convention
is a meaningful direction to test, not evidence that per-cluster phases are
allowed.

The next required check is a frozen full-geometry transfer of the shared +90
degree rule. The present geometry is fixed and all five systems are project-
spent, so the 1.685% local improvement cannot promote the formula.
