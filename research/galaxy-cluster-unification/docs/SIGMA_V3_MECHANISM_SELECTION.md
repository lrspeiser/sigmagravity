## Decision

No Sigma v3 equation is frozen yet.  Two action-derived local scalar geometries
have now failed the same raw cluster-topology gate:

| Action | Exact weak reduction | Galaxy gate | Minimum raw root fraction | Raw topology |
|---|---|:---:|---:|:---:|
| Sigma v1, one nonlinear STEGR scalar | standard-mu AQUAL | pass | 0.333 | fail |
| Sigma v2, second nonmetricity trace | simple QUMOND matter equation | pass | 0.333 | fail |

The remaining information deficit is trace-free and directional.  Sigma v3
must predict where the convergence and two shear components occur, not merely
increase the radial force or introduce a constant metric slip.

The preferred derivation target is a **degenerate, baryon-forced tidal geometry**.
“Degenerate” means its constraints remove unwanted propagating modes;
“baryon-forced” means its complete spatial state is fixed by observed baryons
and one universal boundary prescription; “tidal” means it carries a symmetric
trace-free orientation rather than only a scalar amplitude.

The fallback is a **causal pure-metric nonlocal tidal response**, but only if an
in-in/retarded definition can be given without free homogeneous data or a new
propagator pole.  Ordinary nonlocal MOND with sufficient lensing is already
published prior art, so nonlocality by itself would not be a novel result.

## Why the tempting alternatives are not selected

### Free Aether/vector concentrations

A generalized Aether vector can carry anisotropic stress and can be made
locally stable in restricted coefficient regions.  But published cluster work
obtains displaced lensing by allowing vector-field concentrations that need
not coincide with baryons.  Unless those concentrations are uniquely predicted
from the baryons and a cosmological boundary condition, they are observationally
a hidden halo state.  That violates the central premise even if the field is
called “gravity.”  This prior-art boundary is visible in [Dai, Matsuo, and
Starkman](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4319).

A vector sourced only by baryonic mass current avoids that problem, but its
natural static-cluster signal is velocity suppressed.  Even using a generous
`1500 km/s` baryonic speed gives

\[
{v\over c}=0.00500.
\]

An order-unity linear response would therefore need a coupling of at least
about `200`; a quadratic response would need about `40,000`.  This is a null
control worth deriving only if a nonlinear mechanism simultaneously avoids
preferred-frame Solar-System bounds.

### Naive local tensor

A symmetric trace-free field sourced by baryonic tides has exactly the desired
kind of spatial information.  A component-by-component kinetic term is not
enough, however: an unconstrained relativistic symmetric tensor contains
unphysical modes.  Sigma v3 may use a tensor only after an explicit
Fierz--Pauli, gauge, or degenerate constraint analysis proves the physical
kinetic matrix positive.

### Naively localized retarded field

Writing a nonlocal response with two local auxiliaries often produces the
normalized cross-kinetic matrix

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\lambda(K)=\{-1,+1\}.
\]

That is not a positive-energy two-field fundamental action.  Retarded boundary
conditions can remove independent auxiliary initial data in a causal effective
theory, but then the causal prescription must be derived rather than treating
both auxiliaries as ordinary propagating fields.

### Pure-metric nonlocal scalar MOND

Purely metric, causal nonlocal constructions that reproduce MOND and provide
enhanced lensing already exist; see [Deffayet, Esposito-Farese, and
Woodard](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4984).  They remain an important comparator.
The project would need a distinct, fixed prediction for raw two-dimensional
shear/topology—not a rearrangement of their radial MOND construction—to claim a
new result.

### Degenerate scalar-tensor and minimally modified gravity

Luminal DHOST theories show that second derivatives of a scalar can carry
trace-free information without automatically adding an Ostrogradsky ghost;
the degeneracy and \(c_T=c\) restrictions are known explicitly in [Langlois et
al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07403).  This is a viable mathematical framework,
not yet a Sigma v3 action.  A specific candidate would still have to prove that
its scalar homogeneous mode is fixed by one universal boundary rule and that
its small-scale screening survives inside baryonic matter.

Cuscuton/minimally modified gravity is an instructive constraint-safe control:
it can modify gravity without adding a local propagating scalar mode.  However,
published regular static spherical solutions in two type-II theories coincide
with GR ([De Felice, Mukohyama, and
Pookkillath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14496)), and a scalar constraint alone
does not supply the required trace-free orientation.  It is therefore not the
selected cluster mechanism.

## Hard requirements before Sigma v3 can be frozen

The action candidate must satisfy all of these on paper before any cluster
parameter is exposed:

1. one physical metric for matter and light;
2. baryons plus one universal boundary rule uniquely fix every added state;
3. no freely initialized spatial profile that can act as a halo;
4. an orientation-carrying trace-free response;
5. a covariant action or a rigorously causal effective variational definition;
6. positive physical kinetic and gradient modes with an explicit constraint
   count;
7. luminal transverse tensor propagation structurally, not by an object fit;
8. automatic Solar-System suppression;
9. at most five universal constants; and
10. a weak prediction materially different from the AQUAL and QUMOND closures
    already tested.

The machine-readable matrix is
`results/sigma_v3_mechanism_selection/mechanism_matrix.csv`.  No row presently
passes every requirement; this is why freezing a plausible-looking formula now
would be premature.
