# P0630 seeded synthetic-universe laboratory

> **Scope correction:** P0630 is a gravity-law forward laboratory. Its
> parametric particle scenes were not evidence that the generator reproduces
> real galaxy observables. P0631 adds and validates that observation-matched
> layer; see
> [`P0631_OBSERVATION_MATCHED_REPLICA_RESULTS.md`](P0631_OBSERVATION_MATCHED_REPLICA_RESULTS.md).

## Outcome

P0630 implements the first reusable forward simulator in this research branch.
It can construct deterministic three-dimensional baryonic galaxy and cluster
scenes, swap gravity laws without changing the seed, render radial rotation or
lensing predictions, trace exact raw strong-lens image roots, recover a known
injected law, and run a structured million-system counterfactual population.

The machinery works; the present transport formula does not yet unify the real
observations. With seven universal constants and no per-object gravity setting,
it improves cluster-scale radial summaries substantially but gives up galaxy
accuracy and remains well behind an object-specific compact halo on raw image
positions.

| Locked P0630 holdout | Baryons | Fixed RAR | Simple MOND | Transport | Compact halo |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 23 whole galaxies, equal-galaxy velocity RMSE (km/s) | 52.180 | **23.326** | 23.800 | 29.298 | not evaluated here |
| 4 cluster radial profiles, equal-cluster log RMSE (dex) | 0.8503 | 0.4908 | 0.4863 | **0.1981** | target is already lens-model-derived |
| 2 raw clusters / 6 held-out images, exact RMS (arcsec) | 25.342 | 25.726 | 25.630 | 18.008 | **9.977** |

The transport law is 25.6% worse than fixed RAR on held-back galaxy speeds,
59.6% better than RAR on the derived cluster radial target, and 80.5% worse
than the per-cluster compact-halo raw-image result. It reproduces 64.7% of
held-back galaxy points and 71.7% of held-back cluster radial points to within
20% in their corresponding circular-speed representation.

The raw result also has an additional failure: every held-out image root is
found, but the transport law does not recover every exact training root for
MACS1931. Its 18.008-arcsec number is therefore descriptive, not a passed raw
lensing solution.

## What was built

The simulator keeps four layers separate:

1. **Baryonic seed.** SPARC light/gas force components or Tian cluster baryonic
   acceleration knots define the source. An observed rotation speed or total
   lensing acceleration is not stored in the seed object.
2. **Scene realization.** Exponential stellar/gas disks, Hernquist bulges, and
   profile-sampled cluster particles create deterministic three-dimensional
   realizations. Direct vector summation verifies mass conservation and finite
   forces.
3. **Gravity plugin.** `baryons`, fixed `rar`, `simple_mond`, and `transport`
   operate on the same seed. The plugin interface does not permit an object
   name or target residual to select a gravity constant.
4. **Virtual observation.** Radial accelerations become galaxy rotation speeds
   or spherical photon-deflection fields. The existing exact raw lens solver
   then fits only ordinary lens geometry and source positions on training
   images and traces roots for held-out images.

Real-system scoring uses measured baryonic force knots instead of pretending
that a few thousand Monte Carlo particles reproduce a galaxy's full formation
history. The particle realization is a geometry and counterfactual layer; the
radial knots are the current fidelity layer.

Representative particle scenes conserve their force-equivalent baryonic mass
to floating-point precision and return finite direct forces at every checked
radius. Their direct-force curves differ from the higher-fidelity radial knots
by 0.107--0.355 dex, which quantifies why the radial calibration layer remains
necessary.

## Locked split and leakage controls

The protocol was frozen before P0630 target scoring.

| Domain | Train | Development | Holdout |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Whole SPARC galaxies | 81 | 27 | 23 |
| Cluster radial systems | 12 | 4 | 4 |

The galaxy split is a stable SHA-256 partition of the galaxy name; it never
uses a rotation residual, morphology outcome, or target speed. The four radial
cluster holdouts are A2261, MACS1115, MACS1931, and RXJ2248. MACS1115 and
MACS1931 are also excluded from every P0630 gravity fit before their raw image
positions are scored.

This is a **P0630 replay holdout**, not untouched external validation. All
systems had already appeared somewhere in earlier project work. The immutable
ledger records system names and input hashes in
`results/p0630_synthetic_universe/split_ledger.json`.

## Injected-truth validation

Before opening the real holdouts, the complete fit path was tested on 48
synthetic galaxy/cluster profiles generated by a known transport law. Twelve
entire systems were withheld. With 0.005-dex injected noise, the recovered law
predicted those unseen systems at 0.00491 dex, below the frozen 0.02-dex gate.

The recovered coefficients were also numerically close to their injected
values. This proves that the optimizer, plugin, split, and renderer can recover
this law when it is actually the data-generating process. It does not support
the law as an explanation of the real Universe.

## Tested transport equation

The frozen phenomenological law was

\[
g = g_b
  + A g_b\left(\frac{a_0}{a_0+g_b}\right)^p
    \left[0.2+\frac{0.8}{1+(\Sigma/\Sigma_*)^n}\right]
  + B W(R_{80}/L) g_b(200\,\mathrm{kpc})
    \frac{200\,\mathrm{kpc}}{r}
    \left(\frac{a_0}{a_0+g_b}\right)^p,
\]

where

\[
W(R_{80}/L)=\frac{(R_{80}/L)^2}{1+(R_{80}/L)^2}.
\]

The final pre-holdout fit is:

| Universal quantity | Value | Plain meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| \(a_0\) | \(1.365\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) | low-acceleration switch |
| \(A\) | 4.519 | maximum local diffuse-field contribution |
| \(p\) | 3.622 | sharpness of the acceleration switch |
| \(\Sigma_*\) | 861.3 \(M_\odot\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}\) | surface-density transition |
| \(n\) | 1.822 | sharpness of the surface transition |
| \(L\) | 60.40 kpc | compact-to-extended system transition |
| \(B\) | 8.021 | strength of the returned \(1/r\) field tail |

No parameter is fitted to an individual galaxy or cluster. Lens axis ratio,
orientation, center, external shear, and source positions remain object-level
nuisance geometry rather than gravity constants.

## Million-system counterfactual sweep

The fast scene generator evaluated 1,048,576 scrambled Sobol seeds covering
\(10^7\)--\(10^{12.5}\,M_\odot\), gas fraction, bulge fraction, disk size,
surface density, concentration, and clumpiness. The fitted law's acceleration
boost has median 4.182, 1st--99th percentile 1.002--5.616, and maximum 10.606.

Spearman sensitivity of the boost is:

| Seed property | Correlation with boost |
|---|---:|
| baryonic acceleration | -0.978 |
| surface density | -0.929 |
| total baryonic mass | -0.662 |
| gas fraction | +0.101 |
| bulge fraction | -0.020 |
| clumpiness | +0.007 |
| concentration | +0.005 |

This is an important negative result for the motivating vector-interference
idea. In the current equation, almost all response is controlled by low
acceleration and low surface density. Clumpiness and concentration contribute
negligible independent variation. The fit has found a cross-scale switch, but
not evidence for a distinctive unsummed-vector pattern.

## What the actual curves reveal

The mass-quantile figure selects four held-back galaxies only by baryonic mass,
then displays all four cluster radial holdouts. It shows the failure more
clearly than an aggregate score:

- transport closely follows RAR in small galaxies;
- it becomes too strong for several high-mass held-back galaxies;
- its 60-kpc extent switch supplies the extra cluster amplitude missing from
  RAR/MOND;
- matching radial cluster amplitude is insufficient to place two-dimensional
  strong-lens images correctly.

## Scientific interpretation

The simulator answers the immediate question: yes, we can create controlled
baryonic systems, fit one universal law without per-object gravity settings,
and ask it to generate observations for entirely held-back systems.

It also isolates the present theory problem. One scalar radial field can be
tuned to interpolate between galaxy and cluster amplitude, but that does not
make it a successful lens model. MACS1115 and MACS1931 require different
two-dimensional deflection structure. The next candidate needs a baryon-derived
directional or tensor field that predicts angular topology while reducing to a
RAR-quality radial response in galaxies. Adding more freedom only to this
scalar amplitude would fit the already-known split rather than explain it.
