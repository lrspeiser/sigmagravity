# P0603–P0606: directional-field and cross-domain results

## Outcome

This stage replaced the spherical amplitude multiplier with a positive,
mass-conserving two-dimensional Green kernel driven only by the observed
baryonic source pattern. It found a stable galaxy/cluster morphology
compromise, but that compromise fails when projected spherically into the raw
RX J2129 lens equation.

The central empirical result is therefore a conflict between observables:

- normalized cluster convergence maps prefer a substantial inward-routed
  component;
- galaxy rotation curves prefer a shorter and broader redistribution;
- the raw RX J2129 image likelihood prefers zero routed fraction in the tested
  spherical projection.

This does not rule out two-dimensional routing. It rules out treating a kernel
that improves normalized map shape as though it were automatically the same
physical radial deflection field.

## The tested field operator

Each baryonic source at position `x_i` receives a normalized Green kernel

\[
K_i(\mathbf y)=
\mathcal N\!\left(\mathbf y-\mathbf x_i-L_i\mathbf d_i;
\mathbf D_i\right),
\]

with

\[
L_i=\ell R_{80}
\operatorname{clip}\!\left[
\left(\frac{r_i}{R_{80}}\right)^\nu,0.2,3
\right]
\]

and

\[
\mathbf D_i=(qR_{80})^2
\left[a\,\mathbf e_i\mathbf e_i^T+a^{-1}
(\mathbf I-\mathbf e_i\mathbf e_i^T)\right].
\]

`d_i` mixes the direction toward the baryonic centroid with the acceleration
direction generated by every other baryonic source. `e_i` is a principal axis
of the softened baryonic tidal Hessian. Every kernel integrates to one, so the
operator redistributes baryon-sourced response without inserting an
independent component.

The resulting normalized map is

\[
\kappa_{\rm route}=(1-f_{\rm eff})B_{\rm local}
+f_{\rm eff}\sum_i w_iK_i,
\qquad
f_{\rm eff}=f_{\max}G(C)S_4,
\]

where `C=R50/R80`, `G(C)` is a bounded concentration gate, and `S4` is the
fourth-power acceleration screen. The photon closure solves
`nabla^2 psi=2 kappa_route` and uses `alpha=gradient(psi)`. Spectral tests give
a maximum relative curl of `3.1e-17`, a Poisson residual below `1.4e-16`, and
mass-conservation errors below `7e-16`.

This is a phenomenological Green function, not a covariant action.

## P0603: what matters in two dimensions

Thirty one-at-a-time variants were tested on ten complete cluster holdouts,
using Lenstool best maps for selection and GLAFIC best maps only as a method
control. The same variants were projected axisymmetrically onto 131 SPARC
galaxies and screened in a uniform-Sun proxy.

| Cluster-map ingredient | Equal-JS response span |
|---|---:|
| Travel distance / R80 | 0.02589 |
| Width / R80 | 0.01739 |
| Inward versus external direction | 0.01440 |
| Routed fraction | 0.01104 |
| Tensor axis ratio | 0.00466 |
| Concentration gate | 0.00427 |
| Radial exponent | 0.00312 |
| Tensor orientation | 0.00051 |

The tensor law improves held Lenstool JS by 17.4% versus local-light smoothing
and 12.2% versus a smooth central blob. GLAFIC gives improvements of 16.6% and
20.3%. It nevertheless trails the simpler W060 inward-return control by 11.8%
on Lenstool and 9.1% on GLAFIC.

The selected tensor law is also 12.6% worse than fixed RAR on galaxies. Tidal
orientation is almost irrelevant, stronger anisotropy is adverse, and mixing
toward the external field is adverse. The data favor an isotropic, inward,
scale-controlled kernel rather than the proposed Hessian tensor.

## P0604: the full cross-domain frontier

The unsupported tensor ingredients were removed. A 720-law grid varied only
maximum fraction, travel/R80, width/R80, and concentration gate. Three galaxy
error budgets were imposed before selecting a law inside each whole-cluster
fold.

| Allowed galaxy error | Held Lenstool JS | vs local | vs central | vs W060 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.02 × RAR | 0.07592 | +9.99% | +4.33% | -21.80% |
| 1.05 × RAR | 0.07567 | +10.28% | +4.64% | -21.41% |
| 1.10 × RAR | 0.06675 | +20.86% | +15.89% | -7.09% |

The plus sign denotes lower JS and therefore improvement. The result shows a
continuous tradeoff, not a missed isolated coefficient. Cluster maps favor
larger routed fraction and moderate width; galaxies favor broader kernels and
lower effective fraction.

In this simplified grid, fraction and width have nearly equal cluster impact
(`0.01294` and `0.01220` median-JS span). Width is the largest galaxy control
(`0.856 km/s` median-RMSE span), followed by fraction (`0.479 km/s`). The
concentration gate changes cluster JS by only `0.00162` and galaxy RMSE by only
`0.029 km/s`.

## P0604B: a stable no-galaxy-degradation law

Inspection of the frontier disclosed that 161 candidates beat fixed RAR on
the equal-galaxy metric. A separately labeled post-hoc stability test then
required every eligible candidate to have galaxy RMSE no worse than RAR.

All five whole-cluster folds independently selected

\[
f_{\max}=1,\quad
L=0.25R_{80},\quad
\sigma=0.5R_{80},\quad
f_{\rm eff}=H(C)S_4.
\]

| Test | Fixed/reference | Strict route | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 131 galaxy equal RMSE | 10.716 km/s | 10.502 km/s | 2.00% lower |
| Lenstool held JS vs LOCAL75 | 0.08434 | 0.07466 | 11.48% lower |
| Lenstool held JS vs CENTRAL100 | 0.07936 | 0.07466 | 5.91% lower |
| GLAFIC held JS vs LOCAL75 | 0.08924 | 0.08008 | 10.27% lower |
| GLAFIC held JS vs CENTRAL100 | 0.09330 | 0.08008 | 14.17% lower |
| Lenstool W060 | 0.06233 | 0.07466 | strict route is 19.79% worse |

Only 4/10 Lenstool systems beat LOCAL75 and 6/10 beat CENTRAL100, so the mean
advantage is not a uniform per-cluster victory. The strict budget was motivated
after the P0604 frontier was inspected. The five-fold stability is useful, but
it is not prospective validation.

The fourth-power source screen leaves a Solar activation of
`4.94e-50`; all 720 variants are numerically inactive in the uniform-Sun
interior proxy. No PPN or Cassini metric has been derived.

## P0605–P0606: raw lensing rejects the spherical route

The strict route was frozen and replayed on 15 training and seven spent
held-out RX J2129 image positions with 16 geometry starts and no fitted gravity
parameters.

| Frozen raw closure | Held roots | Held RMS |
|---|---:|---:|
| P0599 without route | 7/7 | 1.809 arcsec |
| Strict route + RAR | 7/7 | 5.043 arcsec |
| Strict route + P0599 amplitude | 4/7 | undefined |
| Earlier project candidate | 7/7 | 1.064 arcsec |
| Compact one-halo control | 7/7 | 2.536 arcsec |
| Published multi-halo reference | — | about 0.29 arcsec |

The strict route moves an effective 74.0% of the spherical response and drives
the fitted geometry to a bound. Adding the potential amplitude overcorrects so
strongly that exact roots are lost.

A 16-variant local sensitivity sweep kept the P0599 amplitude fixed and varied
only routing:

| Raw parameter | Training-RMS response span | Failed-root variants |
|---|---:|---:|
| Routed fraction | 5.412 arcsec | 5/8 |
| Width / R80 | 0.761 arcsec | 2/5 |
| Travel / R80 | 0.071 arcsec | 3/5 |

Training data select zero routing. A 5% maximum route worsens training RMS from
0.495 to 0.651 arcsec, although its already-spent held score happens to improve
from 1.809 to 1.574 arcsec. That held improvement cannot select the law.

Thus routed fraction is by far the most consequential raw-lensing variable.
Width can soften its damage; changing travel length cannot repair it.

## Universal findings from this stage

1. **Scale and fraction dominate.** Travel, width, and routed fraction affect
   real observations much more than tensor orientation or the concentration
   gate.
2. **The proposed tidal tensor is unsupported.** Its orientation has an
   order-of-magnitude smaller cluster effect than travel scale and never helps
   the galaxy projection.
3. **The cross-domain compromise is reproducible on spent map data.** One
   short/broad law is selected in all five folds and modestly improves both
   galaxy curves and simple cluster-map nulls.
4. **Normalized morphology is insufficient.** The same law badly fails the
   integrated raw deflection likelihood.
5. **Raw lensing is hypersensitive to route fraction.** This parameter must be
   predicted from measured baryonic components, not chosen from normalized
   maps.
6. **Solar screening remains easy but incomplete.** `S4` suppresses every
   tested route numerically, but a relativistic metric theory is still absent.

## Why the raw failure is not yet a clean rejection of 2-D routing

P0605 and P0606 project the two-dimensional route into a spherical cumulative
mass profile. That moves gas, BCG, and satellite-equivalent mass radially as a
single component. The map experiment instead routes discrete member-light
sources in two dimensions and scores only normalized shape. These are not the
same observable calculation.

The repository already contains the RX J2129 member catalog
`data/raw/r1_clash_caminha2019/RXJ2129-P1_mcmc/members_lenstool_v2.dat`, HST
F160W imaging, a BCG/ICL model, satellite acceleration products, and X-ray gas
products. The next discriminating calculation is therefore possible locally:

1. construct an absolute two-dimensional baryon map with gas, BCG/ICL, and
   satellites kept as separate components;
2. route only the component and fraction specified by the field law;
3. solve the two-dimensional Poisson lens equation directly;
4. fit only common geometry/source nuisances to the raw images;
5. compare zero route and the predeclared 5% diagnostic route before attempting
   another cluster.

This calculation can determine whether the spherical projection caused the
raw failure. It remains an engineering test on spent RX J2129 data; a different
raw cluster is required for confirmation.
