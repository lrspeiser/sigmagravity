# Sigma v19A assembly-history readiness result

## Result

The spent MACS J0416 / PLCK G287 pair does **not** presently identify one
universal causal-assembly coordinate. No history source is constructed, no
formula is selected, no lensing or halo map is used, and no new target or
holdout is opened.

This is a data-identifiability result, not a falsification of causal-history
physics. It prevents an unmeasured merger-stage parameter from being inserted
into the gravity law merely because it improves the answer.

## What was tested

The frozen protocol first computes two present-epoch controls from the same
target-blind member selection used in v18:

1. the Dressler--Shectman statistic, which asks whether nearby galaxies have
   unusual line-of-sight means or dispersions; and
2. a planar line-of-sight velocity gradient, with its direction tested by
   bootstrap resampling.

Both null distributions are generated by 5,000 deterministic permutations of
the measured velocities over the fixed galaxy positions. The add-one
permutation rule gives a minimum resolvable one-sided probability of
`1/5001`, or only `3.540 sigma`. These controls can show projected substructure;
they cannot distinguish two systems with the same present snapshot but
different past trajectories.

| Present-epoch control | MACS J0416 | PLCK G287 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Selected secure members | 231 | 129 |
| Global line-of-sight dispersion | 1,184.7 km/s | 1,823.5 km/s |
| Dressler--Shectman `Delta/N` | 1.4066 | 1.0458 |
| DS permutation significance | 2.636 sigma | 0.096 sigma |
| Velocity-gradient magnitude | 848.2 km/s/Mpc | 441.0 km/s/Mpc |
| Gradient permutation significance | 3.353 sigma | -0.796 sigma |
| Gradient direction east of north | 354.6 degrees | 100.2 degrees |
| Bootstrap 68% absolute direction error | 10.0 degrees | 70.9 degrees |

MACS J0416 therefore contains a stable projected velocity direction, but PLCK
G287 does not show a transferable counterpart. Neither statistic is a clock.

## Why the external clock evidence does not close the gate

For MACS J0416, [Jauzac et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.3011) discussed both
a near-first-passage scenario and a prior-passage, turnaround, second-approach
scenario. A deeper Chandra/JVLA analysis by
[Ogrean et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05560) instead interpreted the main
approximately 250 kpc pair as pre-merger. Its detected density discontinuity,
approximately 450 kpc south-southwest of the southwest component, was linked
to a smaller interaction rather than a propagation clock for the main pair.
The primary event therefore has no unique measured time coordinate.

For PLCK G287, [Gitti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13735) measured a
`389 +/- 6 kpc` shock, density jump `1.43 +/- 0.10`, Mach number
`1.29 +/- 0.07`, and approximate speed of `2,100 km/s`. The corresponding
approximately `180 Myr` value is an upper bound under an AGN-driven
interpretation, while the paper leaves merger and AGN origins unresolved. It
is a real propagation diagnostic but not a unique time since cluster core
passage. The density jump above unity is `4.3 sigma`, below the frozen
five-sigma history gate in any event.

The local derived member tables also omit redshift-error columns, the present
bundle lacks matched resolved-temperature uncertainty maps, and neither system
has measured transverse member velocities or line-of-sight depths from which
to build the declared projection ensemble.

## Implication for the root equation and halo size

The intended causal theory class remains schematic:

\[
\mathcal D_{\rm causal}[g,T_b]\,\mathcal S_{\mu\nu}
=\mathcal P_{\mu\nu}[T_b,\nabla T_b],
\qquad
G_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal K_{\mu\nu}[g,\mathcal S]
={8\pi G\over c^4}T^{(b)}_{\mu\nu}.
\]

The apparent dark halo must be computed from the resulting physical metric.
Its extent, centroid, and orientation are outputs; a merger age, halo radius,
or response length cannot be fitted for each object.

The experiments so far say that instantaneous baryonic density, component
geometry, thermal stress, and member random stress contain useful information
about the *extent* of the required field. They do not fix its universal
amplitude, phase, and shear. Assembly history could in principle supply that
missing state information, but this pair cannot measure it independently.
Accordingly, history is not added to the root equation at this stage.

## Next evidence required

A replacement spent sample must contain at least two systems, and ultimately
four before holdout, with the same source-side measurements:

- a shock or cold-front radius, propagation speed, and uncertainty tied
  uniquely to the primary merger;
- member position/redshift uncertainties plus an explicit transverse-velocity
  and line-of-sight-depth ensemble;
- resolved gas-density and temperature uncertainty maps;
- BCG, intracluster-light, stellar, and gas maps registered on one WCS; and
- enough contrast in measured merger phase to test the equal-snapshot,
  different-history sentinel.

Only after those measurements pass target-blind readiness may one universal
source be frozen and transferred against the already-spent lensing maps. The
machine-readable result is
`results/sigma_v19a_assembly_history_readiness/report.json`.
