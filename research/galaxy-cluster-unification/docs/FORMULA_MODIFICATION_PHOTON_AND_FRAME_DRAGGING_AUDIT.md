# Formula-modification, photon-path, and frame-dragging audit

## Bottom line

The project has not yet found a root equation.  The authoritative registry now
contains 128 scientifically distinct scored laws, 138 explicit formula/action
fragments across 123 protocols, and 36 directly overlapping published
families.  The broad empirical winner is still

\[
{g\over g_{\rm bar}}=E_{\rm RAR}
+[1-(3C^2-2C^3)]^2[\epsilon(\rho_b)^{-1}-1],
\]

but this is RAR plus a smooth density/coherence response, not a derivation from
one action.  It is close to fixed MOND on held-out SPARC data and much closer
than fixed MOND to NFW-derived cluster radial fields, yet no universal
candidate has passed raw multi-cluster image topology and positions.

## How many situations are still difficult?

There are four immediate observational problems and two later theory-wide
problems.

| Gate | Current state | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Held-out galaxy rotation, including morphology groups | empirically close to MOND; some independent dwarf results are better | derive the response rather than importing RAR; retain performance across disks, bulges, dwarfs, gas-rich and low-surface-brightness systems |
| Raw strong lensing in several clusters | failed as a universal theory | all image roots, correct multiplicity/caustics, centroid, ellipticity and shear direction with one frozen setting |
| Baryons to apparent-halo scale/phase transfer | current V19 causal-assembly test | predict extent, centroid and orientation from gas, galaxies, shocks and assembly time without a fitted halo radius or direction |
| Same-object matter and photon response | incomplete | one metric must predict both member/gas dynamics and lensing in the same systems |
| Local/relativistic health | analytic Solar proxies pass for some candidates, but no survivor is complete | full PPN/ephemeris likelihood, luminal tensor propagation, positive kinetic/gradient energy and a well-posed initial-value problem |
| Weak lensing and cosmology | not passed | reduced shear, magnification, time delays, structure growth and background evolution from the same constants |

Thus the concise answer is **four immediate scenarios still block the root
equation, and six when the full theory burden is counted**.  Galaxy radial
amplitude is no longer the largest obstacle.  Spatial cluster curvature and a
healthy common metric are.

## Other source and propagation details we must keep in scope

The investigation should not reduce every system to total baryonic mass.  A
relativistic source and an astronomical measurement contain several distinct
pieces of information.

| Information | Relativistic role | Why it may distinguish galaxies and clusters | Current treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest mass and internal energy | principally `T_00`; sources the gravitoelectric potentials | controls ordinary radial attraction | already present in baryon maps |
| Baryonic mass current and angular momentum | `T_0i`; sources gravitomagnetic/frame-dragging terms | coherent disks and multistream cluster mergers have very different current topology | not yet scored as a universal full-field response; added below |
| Isotropic pressure | spatial trace of `T_ij` | hot intracluster gas carries much more pressure per unit rest mass than cold disk matter | tested in V17 pressure/stress lanes; no transferable metric response yet |
| Anisotropic and multistream stress | traceless part of `T_ij` | merger shocks, member velocity dispersion and intersecting flows contain direction | current V19 thermodynamic/member phase measurement |
| Time dependence and causal history | time derivatives and retarded field propagation | two systems with the same present density can have different assembly phase | current V19 shock/clock lane |
| Density gradients, tidal field and topology | derivatives/invariants of the baryonic field | a thin disk, bulge, dwarf and porous multi-centre cluster need not redirect a nonlinear field alike | many tensor/coherence/path tests; no survivor |
| Line-of-sight structure and foregrounds | projection of source and metric | can shift apparent centroid/shear without new gravity | explicit projection covariance required before scoring |
| Plasma, dust and instrumental propagation | ordinary electromagnetic nuisance, generally chromatic | can alter flux, phase, dispersion or shape but should not imitate achromatic gravity silently | must be modeled/control-tested, never absorbed into a gravity constant |
| Photon phase, polarization and coherence | Maxwell/QED propagation on or beyond the metric | could change interference, time delay or image formation even if mass motion is unchanged | simple illusion limits failed; nonlocal proposal below remains untested |

This is also a useful first-principles decomposition: `T_00`, `T_0i`, and
`T_ij` are not arbitrary object labels.  They are the energy, current, and
stress components of one baryonic stress-energy tensor.  A promising root
equation should respond to them covariantly rather than switching between a
disk formula and a cluster formula.

The important negative result is consistent across these tests: changing only
an amplitude, exponent, or transition radius can often repair a radial curve,
but it rarely creates the correct two-dimensional Hessian of the lensing
potential.  That is why V19 is measuring causal, directional baryonic state.

## What was actually tested about a photon/QED illusion?

Three simple, calculable limits were tested against observations.

| Test | Mathematical change | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Same-source Milky Way masers | additive photon-frequency velocity term, constant or low-acceleration dependent | best constant amplitude 0.218 km/s; 95% interval -5.876 to 6.027 km/s; no support for the required tens of km/s |
| SPARC propagation distance | unsaturated logarithmic growth of photon bias with source distance | -0.233 km/s per distance decade; 95% interval -7.776 to 7.299 km/s; no unsaturated accumulation |
| M33 direct angular versus H I rotation | photon spectroscopic excess relative to geometric proper motion | H I minus astrometric rotation -9.93 km/s; 95% interval -59.62 to 39.59 km/s; a 61.4 km/s illusion has probability 0.00231 under that model |

These results rule out the simple frequency-only and unsaturated-distance
versions.  They do **not** calculate the stronger proposal in which path
amplitudes preserve environment-dependent phase or coherence.

## A calculable nonlocal photon-path proposal

For a set of unresolved propagation paths `p`, write the received amplitude as

\[
\mathcal A(x_o)=\sum_p a_p\exp[i\varphi_p],
\]

and the intensity as

\[
I=\sum_p|a_p|^2+
\sum_{p\ne q}D_{pq}\,a_pa_q^*
\exp[i(\varphi_p-\varphi_q)].
\]

Standard geometric lensing is recovered when rapidly varying cross terms
average away and stationary phase selects the classical null paths.  A precise
version of the proposed new physics can be written

\[
\varphi_p=\varphi_p^{\rm GR}
+\kappa_Q\int_p \mathcal F(I_b,T_b,\nabla T_b,\ldots)\,d\lambda,
\]

\[
D_{pq}=\exp\left[-\int\Gamma_Q(p,q;I_b,T_b)\,d\lambda\right],
\]

where `I_b` denotes baryonic curvature/density/current invariants and
`kappa_Q` is universal.  Merely changing `D_pq` changes interference and
magnification but generally not geometric image locations.  Moving images
requires the new phase to alter the stationary-path equation

\[
\delta(\varphi^{\rm GR}+\Delta\varphi_Q)=0.
\]

The stronger field form is a retarded nonlocal Maxwell equation,

\[
\nabla_\nu F^{\nu\mu}(x)
+\kappa_Q\int d^4x'\sqrt{-g(x')}
K^\mu{}_{\nu}(x,x';I_b,T_b)A^\nu(x')=0.
\]

This is now mathematical enough to simulate, but it is not yet a theory.  The
kernel must be transverse to preserve gauge invariance, retarded to preserve
causality, stable, and specified without a cluster label.  It must predict
frequency, polarization, time-delay, image-position and interference effects
together.  Because it modifies photons alone, it can explain galaxy rotation
only as an observational illusion; the existing maser and M33 tests already
make the simplest versions difficult.

## A calculable baryonic frame-dragging lane

Ordinary frame dragging is already present in linearized GR.  With
`bar(h)_mn` the trace-reversed metric perturbation,

\[
\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}=-{16\pi G\over c^4}T_{\mu\nu}.
\]

Rest density principally sources `bar(h)_00`; baryonic mass current
`j_i=rho v_i` sources `bar(h)_0i`.  The latter is the gravitomagnetic or
Lense-Thirring channel.  Massive bodies and photons both follow the resulting
metric, so this is naturally compatible with the one-metric requirement.

A one-parameter diagnostic can hold the scalar metric fixed and test

\[
\bar h_{0i}=\left[1+\kappa_J S(I_b)\right]
\bar h_{0i}^{\rm GR}[\rho_b\mathbf v_b],
\]

where `S(I_b)` is one universal high-acceleration screen and `kappa_J` is one
universal current susceptibility.  This is not yet covariant—the selective
rescaling could violate the metric constraints—so it is only a falsifiable
weak-field diagnostic.  If data favored it, the next step would be a conserved
vector/tensor action whose weak limit produces the same response.

The required inputs are baryonic mass-current maps, not just density maps:

- stellar and gas rotation fields for galaxies, including vertical structure;
- cluster member positions, masses and velocity uncertainties;
- gas bulk-flow or line-shift constraints where available; and
- projection covariances for unmeasured transverse velocities.

The primary tests are whether the same `kappa_J` improves disk rotation and
raw cluster image roots, whether its predicted curl/shear orientation matches
lensing, and whether Solar frame-dragging and PPN constraints remain satisfied.
The GR value is the mandatory zero-extra-response control.  A null result would
reject the tested susceptibility, not the existence of ordinary
Lense-Thirring gravity.

## Long-wavelength gravity mode: explicitly included, but not new

The hypothesis that gravity has an additional wavelength longer than a Solar
System has a clean mathematical meaning: the locally measured metric response
is the high-wavenumber limit, while an extra metric mode becomes coherent only
at wavelengths comparable to a universal length `L_Sigma`.  A representative
one-metric transfer is

\[
 \widetilde{\bar h}_{\mu\nu}(k)=
 \widetilde{\bar h}^{\rm GR}_{\mu\nu}(k)
 \exp\!\left[A\exp\!\left(-(kL_\Sigma)^2\right)\right].
\]

For `k L_Sigma >> 1`, as for variation across a star system when
`L_Sigma` is galactic, the exponential tends to one and local gravity is
unchanged.  For wavelengths of order `L_Sigma` or longer it tends toward
`exp(A)`, so the same baryonic source produces a larger long-scale metric
response.  Both matter and light see the resulting metric; this is not a
lens-only multiplier.  The two universal quantities are the strength `A` and
the coherence length `L_Sigma`.

This exact concept was already made quantitative in Sigma V3B and then tested
as an isotropic wavelength-only operator in V3C.  It could separate Solar,
galaxy and cluster scales and it generated nonzero shear on a manufactured
map.  It did not reproduce the full convergence and two-shear maps across the
two spent clusters: the joint normalized Fourier error was `0.800` against a
frozen `0.500` plausibility gate, cross-cluster nonparametric transfer errors
were `0.800--0.956`, and the required transfer had low radial coherence and
negative bins.  A separate fractional long-wavelength lensing test also failed
to transfer between SMACS J0723 and SPT-CL J0615 after its apparent gain was
identified as a mass-sheet/source-plane degeneracy.

So the long-wavelength premise remains in the idea inventory, but the already
tested **linear, isotropic, wavelength-only** realization is not a surviving
root equation.  A materially new version would have to let wavelength
coherence depend covariantly on baryonic current, stress, or tidal orientation;
changing only `A`, `L_Sigma`, or a Fourier power would repeat the closed tests.

## Decision rule for these additions

1. Do not alter the frozen V19H source measurement protocol.
2. After V19H produces member/gas phase covariances, construct a baryonic
   mass-current map and score the GR frame-dragging control before fitting
   `kappa_J`.
3. Preregister one universal screened `kappa_J` diagnostic and evaluate it on
   galaxies and development clusters, then freeze it before a holdout.
4. Treat the nonlocal photon kernel as a separate experimental lane.  It must
   first pass same-source astrometry/spectroscopy, chromaticity, polarization
   and time-delay tests before it can be used on cluster image positions.
5. Neither lane can be combined with the empirical RAR/RG bridge merely because
   they repair different failures.  A combination advances only if one action
   derives the scalar, current, and photon limits with the same constants.
6. Retain the long-wavelength premise only in a directional or source-state
   dependent form.  The linear isotropic wavelength-only realization has
   already failed its cross-cluster map gate and must not be recreated by
   renaming `A`, `L_Sigma`, or the Fourier exponent.
