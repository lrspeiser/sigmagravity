# Directed Source–Target String Model

## 1. Exact claim being tested

The central hypothesis is stronger than generic nonlocality:

> When an extended baryonic source and a distant baryonic target define a long, relatively open
> corridor, contributions from many source elements can align into a shared channel. Gravitational
> response that would otherwise spread across many directions is redistributed into that channel,
> increasing the mutual attraction of the two extended systems.

The distant target does not need to intercept photons first. It participates in a nonlocal or
retarded source–target solution. Changes to either endpoint must still propagate causally.

This document calls the channel a **directed string**, while leaving its radial spreading law open.
A literal fixed-width string, a disk-confined ribbon, and an anisotropic inverse-square field make
different predictions and must not be conflated.

Ordinary gravitational field lines are a visualization, not material pipes carrying a conserved
amount of pull. General relativity does not supply a pipe-like flux budget that matter can redirect
in this way. Any extra channel attraction is therefore a new interaction or field configuration and
must supply its own conserved energy and stress accounting.

## 2. Gravity, light-seeded, and radiative-energy variants

The same source–target geometry supports three distinct hypotheses:

| ID | Channel capacity | Role of light | Immediate constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DS-G` | baryonic mass-energy | none required | must derive a new gravitational correlation field |
| `DS-H` | baryonic mass-energy | identifies or phase-seeds the retarded corridor | must explain why unrelated stellar emission yields a stable collective order parameter |
| `DS-E` | radiative energy in transit | carries both corridor information and source energy | cannot exceed the `LR/c³` mass-equivalent budget without an explicit interaction-energy term |

`DS-G` and `DS-H` map to the mass-normalized `HM` tests. `DS-E` maps to `HL-energy`. They share
geometry and conservation tests but may not exchange normalizations after results are known.

## 3. What the scalar BLC model was missing

The scalar `Q(x)` in `MODEL_SPEC.md` measures how much boundary-linked response is available at a
location. By itself it does not require:

- selection of a particular distant target;
- parallel alignment of paths from many interior sources;
- enhanced mutual attraction along the source–target axis;
- an equal reaction on the interior source distribution; or
- compensating suppression away from the selected channel.

The directed-string model adds those requirements. `Q(x)` remains useful as the local activation
envelope, but it is not the complete string hypothesis.

## 4. Endpoints are extended mass basins, not selected test particles

If a force selected the most massive individual star at a radius, stars of different mass could
fall differently. The model instead partitions the baryonic density into equal-mass source cells
and then aggregates them into extended basins. Each cell has the same link capacity. A large target
attracts more total channel capacity because it contains more cells, not because its individual
constituents receive a different acceleration law. Adaptive unequal-mass cells are a later
numerical generalization and must converge to the equal-mass continuum limit.

All small test bodies must still follow the same effective metric. Interactions between two
self-gravitating extended bodies may violate the strong equivalence principle and therefore require
separate compact-body and external-field tests.

A tracer used to measure the resulting field is not included as a new target cell in the allocation.
It reads the field generated by the source and extended-target configuration. Equivalently, take its
mass to zero before evaluating the acceleration. Otherwise the predicted field could change merely
because a heavier test particle was chosen.

## 5. Collective alignment

For source basin `A` and target basin `B`, define the direction from source element `x` to the target
centroid `y_B` and a collective order parameter

\[
\mathbf s_{A\rightarrow B}=
\frac{1}{M_A}\int_A d^3x\,\rho_b(\mathbf x)
\widehat{(\mathbf y_B-\mathbf x)},
\qquad
C^{\rm align}_{AB}=|\mathbf s_{A\rightarrow B}|^2.
\]

`C_align` approaches one when the target is far enough away that paths from many source elements
are nearly parallel. It is smaller when candidate endpoints surround or interpenetrate the source.
The symmetric alignment score is

\[
C_{AB}=\sqrt{C^{\rm align}_{AB}C^{\rm align}_{BA}}.
\]

A path-survival or openness factor `O_AB` then measures whether competing nearby mass, high
acceleration, or disordered motion closes the corridor locally. `O_AB` must be symmetric and
computed without using the dynamical residual being predicted.

## 6. Raw channel affinity

For baryonic cells or basins `i` and `j`, define a dimensionless symmetric affinity

\[
a_{ij}=K_R(r_{ij})\,C_{ij}\,O_{ij},
\qquad a_{ij}=a_{ji},\qquad a_{ii}=0.
\]

- `K_R` controls the allowed range and is not assumed to be electromagnetic attenuation.
- `C_ij` measures collective directional and kinematic alignment.
- `O_ij` measures corridor survival or lack of local closure.

Target mass is not raised to an extra fitted power in `a_ij`. Its ability to collect links enters
through its total baryonic capacity.

## 7. Fixed channel budget

Split the density into equal baryonic mass quanta `δm` and give each cell capacity `q₀`. Construct a
symmetric allocation matrix

\[
P_{ij}=u_i\,a_{ij}\,u_j,
\]

where the positive balancing factors `u_i` are solved so that

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}=q_0,
\qquad P_{ij}=P_{ji}.
\]

The aggregate channel between extended basins is

\[
P_{AB}=\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}P_{ij}=P_{BA}.
\]

The total link capacity of a basin is `N_A q₀`, proportional to its baryonic mass because
`N_A δm = M_A`. This is a symmetric matrix-balancing problem. If the balancing does not exist, or
if aggregate predictions do not converge as `δm` is reduced, the candidate model fails.

Define an isotropic allocation `P_iso` using the same masses, range kernel, and row budgets but
without alignment or openness preferences. The redistributed channel contrast is

\[
\Delta P_{ij}=P_{ij}-P^{\rm iso}_{ij},
\qquad
\sum_j\Delta P_{ij}=0.
\]

Thus a stronger source–target string must be paid for by weaker allocation to other directions. The
model aligns a fixed budget; it does not create unlimited gravitational charge or `N²` power.

## 8. Conservative interaction family

Define the dimensionless link contrast

\[
\ell_{ij}=\frac{\Delta P_{ij}}
{P^{\rm iso}_{ij}+\epsilon}.
\]

The candidate link energy is

\[
U_{\rm link}=\alpha G
\sum_{i<j}m_i m_j\,\ell_{ij}\,\mathcal V_p(r_{ij}),
\]

with

\[
\frac{d\mathcal V_p}{dr}
=\frac{1}{\ell_*^{\,2-p}r^p}.
\]

Explicitly,

\[
\mathcal V_p(r)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{r^{1-p}}{(1-p)\ell_*^{\,2-p}}, & p\ne1,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{\ln(r/\ell_*)}{\ell_*}, & p=1.
\end{cases}
\]

The discrete hypotheses are:

| `p` | Added-force scaling | Geometric interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| 2 | `1/r²` | angular redistribution with ordinary three-dimensional spreading |
| 1 | `1/r` | ribbon, cylindrical, or effectively two-dimensional spreading; compatible with flat outer rotation curves |
| 0 | constant | fixed-cross-section, literal string tension |

The force is the gradient of the **complete** energy,

\[
\mathbf F_i=-\nabla_{\mathbf x_i}U_{\rm link}.
\]

Because `P`, `u`, `C`, and `O` depend on all positions, their derivatives are part of the force.
Holding the allocation fixed while differentiating would generally violate conservation. The
implementation must use analytic differentiation, automatic differentiation, or converged finite
differences of the total energy.

## 9. Connection to canonical Σ-Gravity

The directed model does not automatically reproduce the canonical enhancement. Its continuum disk
limit must be measured:

\[
\mathbf g_{\rm link}(R,\phi)
=-\frac{1}{m_{\rm test}}\nabla U_{\rm link}.
\]

Decompose it into radial and non-radial harmonics. The radial average is compared with

\[
g_{\Sigma,0}=A(L)\,\mathcal C\,h(g_N)\,g_N.
\]

The model succeeds as an explanation of the canonical formula only if its radial component produces
the same acceleration dependence and outer scaling without per-galaxy parameters. The angular
component is the new prediction that distinguishes a target-seeking channel from the existing
radial window.

## 10. Decisive synthetic configurations

### One distant target

Place an extended source at the origin and one target at distance `D`. Confirm that interior paths
align toward the target, that the total attraction increases along the axis, and that the target and
source receive equal and opposite total forces.

### Rotate or remove the target

The channel and force enhancement must rotate with the target and disappear when it is removed. A
radial-only model is the negative control.

### Two equal opposite targets

The allocation must form two symmetric channels with no unexplained center-of-mass acceleration or
arbitrary winner selection.

### Unequal targets

Vary target baryonic mass at fixed geometry. Total channel capacity may scale with target mass, but
the acceleration of small test tracers in the resulting field must remain composition- and
mass-independent.

### Intervening closure

Insert dense or disordered matter across the corridor. The preregistered openness rule must weaken,
redirect, or terminate the channel without using the measured force as an input.

### Distance and resolution sweeps

Sweep separation to infer the force exponent and repeat at fixed continuous density with increasing
particle count. This distinguishes `p=2`, `p=1`, and `p=0` and exposes hidden `N²` growth.

## 11. Live-data tests that actually measure the string claim

One-dimensional rotation curves are necessary but not sufficient because they average over the
directional signal. The first observational target should combine:

1. **Galaxy-pair center-of-mass dynamics:** baryonic masses, projected separations, relative
   velocities, and isolation cuts test whether attraction along the pair axis exceeds the canonical
   baryonic prediction with the preregistered distance law.
2. **Resolved two-dimensional velocity fields:** approaching/receding and azimuthal residuals in
   galaxies with known companions test whether an internal channel harmonic points toward the
   companion.
3. **Matched isolated controls:** the same mass, surface brightness, inclination, gas fraction, and
   data quality without a comparable external target.
4. **Lensing or dynamical bridges:** where available, compare the predicted channel axis and width
   with mass-map residuals after subtracting baryons and conventional halo/tidal models.

### Initial data ladder

1. **SPARC screening:** verify the radial average and reject parameterizations that fail the
   existing galaxy baseline. This cannot confirm target selection.
2. **MaNGA DR17 primary directional test:** use public stellar and emission-line velocity and
   dispersion maps, the official close-pairs targeting flags, independently measured companion
   positions, and matched non-pair controls. Start with a small quality-controlled download before
   scaling to the full survey.
3. **LITTLE THINGS low-mass confirmation:** use public high-resolution H I moment and velocity maps
   plus the survey's UV/optical/infrared data for nearby dwarfs, crossmatched to independently known
   companions and environment.
4. **Independent pair dynamics or lensing:** reserve a catalog not used to choose `p`, `K_R`, or
   `O_ij` for the confirmatory separation-law and bridge test.

Ordinary Newtonian tides, MOND external-field effects, halo bridges, gas flows, warps, and selection
bias can all create environment correlations. The string model must predict a distinct combination
of separation scaling, angular harmonic, corridor openness, and reaction force.

For a distant target, ordinary internal tidal acceleration scales approximately as

\[
\Delta g_{\rm tide}\sim \frac{GM_B R}{D^3}.
\]

A directed channel with force exponent `p` generically produces a different differential scaling,

\[
\Delta g_{\rm string}\propto
\frac{GM_B R}{\ell_*^{\,2-p}D^{p+1}},
\]

modulated by alignment, openness, and the fixed-budget allocation. The separation exponent and the
target-aligned angular residual are therefore primary observables.

## 12. Falsification conditions

Reject this directed-string mechanism if any of the following holds:

- no symmetric fixed-budget allocation exists for realistic source distributions;
- total force, torque, or energy fails to close when the full allocation is differentiated;
- enhancement grows with numerical particle count at fixed density;
- a large target changes individual test-body acceleration by composition or mass;
- no member of the discrete `p={0,1,2}` family can reproduce the canonical galaxy limit;
- directional predictions vanish after subtracting ordinary tides and survey systematics;
- a fit requires choosing the target, corridor, exponent, or screening rule after seeing residuals;
- the same global parameters fail compact-system, lensing, or propagation constraints.

Passing these tests would not by itself prove a fundamental string field. It would show that the
specific target-seeking, flux-aligning mechanism has a conservative mathematical realization and
survives observations strongly enough to justify a covariant field-theory derivation.
