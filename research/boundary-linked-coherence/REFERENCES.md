# Primary References

These sources define analogies, data, or constraints. None is evidence that boundary-linked
coherence exists.

## Repository data and empirical targets

1. F. Lelli, S. S. McGaugh, and J. M. Schombert, “SPARC: Mass Models for 175 Disk Galaxies with
   Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves,” *Astronomical Journal* **152**, 157 (2016).
   [Publisher-hosted manuscript](https://astroweb.case.edu/ssm/papers/AJv152n157.pdf) — defines
   the primary galaxy mass models and their observational range.

2. S. S. McGaugh, F. Lelli, and J. M. Schombert, “Radial Acceleration Relation in Rotationally
   Supported Galaxies,” *Physical Review Letters* **117**, 201101 (2016).
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.201101) — motivates galaxy-level holdouts and
   strict control for baryonic acceleration.

3. C. Fox, G. Mahler, K. Sharon, and J. D. Remolina González, “The Strongest Cluster Lenses,”
   *Astrophysical Journal* **928**, 87 (2022).
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ac5024) — source for the cluster catalog already used
   to calibrate the repository's path-length exponent; it must not be relabeled as wholly
   independent validation.

## Closest theoretical analogies

4. J. A. Wheeler and R. P. Feynman, “Interaction with the Absorber as the Mechanism of Radiation,”
   *Reviews of Modern Physics* **17**, 157 (1945).
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.17.157) — a source–absorber analogy along relativistic
   paths. It is electrodynamics, not a model of enhanced galactic gravity.

5. S. Rahvar and B. Mashhoon, “Observational Tests of Nonlocal Gravity: Galaxy Rotation Curves
   and Clusters of Galaxies,” *Physical Review D* **89**, 104011 (2014).
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4819) — demonstrates how a causal nonlocal constitutive
   kernel can mimic effective dark matter and provides a comparison standard for kernel
   normalization, lensing, and Solar-System limits.

6. M. Roshan and B. Mashhoon, “Nonlocal Gravity: Modification of Newtonian Gravitational Force in
   the Solar System,” *Universe* **8**, 470 (2022).
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13276) — useful for checking how a galaxy-scale nonlocal
   force is constrained at Solar-System distances.

## Environment and propagation constraints

7. K.-H. Chae, H. Desmond, F. Lelli, S. S. McGaugh, and J. M. Schombert, “Testing the Strong
   Equivalence Principle. II. Relating the External Field Effect in Galaxy Rotation Curves to the
   Large-Scale Structure of the Universe,” *Astrophysical Journal* **921**, 104 (2021).
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04745) — supplies an existing SPARC/environment methodology
   and important confounding controls. BLC must predict more than a generic scalar external-field
   correlation.

8. B. P. Abbott et al. (LIGO Scientific and Virgo Collaborations), “Tests of General Relativity
   with GW170817,” *Physical Review Letters* **123**, 011102 (2019).
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.123.011102) — constrains new propagation and
   dispersion effects in any relativistic completion.

9. B. Bertotti, L. Iess, and P. Tortora, “A Test of General Relativity Using Radio Links with the
   Cassini Spacecraft,” *Nature* **425**, 374–376 (2003).
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01997) — anchors the compact-system post-Newtonian gate.

## Direction-preserving observational data

10. Abdurro'uf et al., “The Seventeenth Data Release of the Sloan Digital Sky Surveys: Complete
    Release of MaNGA, MaStar, and APOGEE-2 Data,” *Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series* (2022).
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/ac4414) and
    [official MaNGA DAP documentation](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/manga/manga-analysis-pipeline/) —
    provides resolved stellar and emission-line velocity and dispersion maps for the primary
    directional test.

11. S. Feng, S.-Y. Shen, F.-T. Yuan, R. A. Riffel, and K. Pan, “SDSS-IV MaNGA: Kinematic
    Asymmetry as an Indicator of Galaxy Interaction in Paired Galaxies,” *Astrophysical Journal
    Letters* (2020). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ab7dba) — establishes a relevant
    paired-galaxy kinematic methodology and, importantly, the ordinary-interaction confound that a
    directed-string test must outperform.

12. D. A. Hunter et al., “LITTLE THINGS,” *Astronomical Journal* **144**, 134 (2012).
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/144/5/134) — provides public high-resolution H I maps
    and multiwavelength observations for a lower-mass directional confirmation sample.

## Reading rule

References are added because they directly constrain a preregistered test or define a comparison.
Analogies should not be cited as derivations, and observational data already used for calibration
should remain labeled as calibration throughout this track.
